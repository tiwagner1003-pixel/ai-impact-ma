# Cadbury Report

**The 1992 UK corporate governance code (formally: *Report of the Committee on the Financial Aspects of Corporate Governance*, chaired by Sir Adrian Cadbury) that established voluntary standards for board accountability, audit committees, and director independence; cited by Howson (2003) for its recommendation that boards review significant acquisitions against a formal checklist.**

## Overview

The Cadbury Report (December 1992) was produced by a committee chaired by Sir Adrian Cadbury and sponsored by the Financial Reporting Council, the London Stock Exchange, and the accountancy profession. It responded to high-profile corporate failures (BCCI, Maxwell) by recommending a code of best practice for UK listed company boards, focusing on the separation of chairman/CEO roles, independent non-executive directors, and audit committee oversight.

In the M&A context, Howson (2003) cites the Cadbury Report's recommendation that boards formally review all significant acquisitions against a structured checklist as the closest thing to a governance obligation for due diligence in UK law. This recommendation is a soft obligation under the "comply or explain" regime of UK corporate governance, not a statutory requirement. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]

The report is an important reference point for the argument that DD is a board-level governance obligation — not merely a commercial practice — even in the absence of a specific statutory DD requirement in most jurisdictions.

## Key facts

- Published December 1992; chaired by Sir Adrian Cadbury. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- Recommended boards review significant acquisitions against a formal checklist — the closest UK governance equivalent to a DD obligation. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- Operates under "comply or explain": listed companies must either follow the code or publicly explain departures; not a statutory DD requirement.
- Preceded the UK Corporate Governance Code (formerly the Combined Code), which consolidated subsequent governance recommendations.
