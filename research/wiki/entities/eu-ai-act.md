# EU AI Act

**The European Union's comprehensive regulation of AI systems (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), establishing a risk-based framework that imposes binding requirements — including accuracy, explainability, and human oversight — on "high-risk" AI systems.**

## Overview

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is the primary EU-level regulatory framework for artificial intelligence. It defines AI systems in Article 3(1) as "machine-based systems designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infer, from the input they receive, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments." This definition — grounded in independence and autonomy rather than the older anthropocentric notion of simulating human intelligence — is adopted by Herbosch & Mertens (2025) as the working definition for M&A AI analysis. [[2026-05-04-herbosch-mertens-2025-risk-allocation-ai-ma]]

The Act's risk-based approach categorizes AI systems as unacceptable-risk (prohibited), high-risk (regulated with binding requirements), limited-risk, or minimal-risk. High-risk systems must meet requirements including accuracy (Article 15), explainability/transparency (Article 13), and human oversight (Article 14). Herbosch & Mertens (2025) note that M&A due diligence AI tools likely fall in the low-risk category under the Act's current Annex III categories, though this classification is not certain for all use cases (e.g., AI used in employment contexts at the target company). [[2026-05-04-herbosch-mertens-2025-risk-allocation-ai-ma]]

Crucially, even where the EU AI Act does not directly apply (low-risk systems), the paper argues that its standards for high-risk systems function as an informal benchmark for what constitutes reasonable care in AI tool selection and deployment — useful for contextualizing due care assessments in all five jurisdictions analysed. [[2026-05-04-herbosch-mertens-2025-risk-allocation-ai-ma]]
