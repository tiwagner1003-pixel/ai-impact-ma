# New York State Department of Financial Services

**New York's primary financial services regulator; filed federal litigation challenging the OCC's Fintech Charter Decision and was cited by Johnson et al. (2019) as a model for nimble state-level consumer-protection enforcement in the algorithmic age.**

## Overview

The New York DFS initiated a federal lawsuit seeking a declaratory judgment and injunction preventing the OCC from issuing SPNB charters to nondepository fintech firms, arguing the OCC exceeded its authority under the National Bank Act. The Southern District of New York initially dismissed DFS's claims as unripe but later denied the OCC's motion to dismiss after the OCC formally adopted the Fintech Charter Decision, finding that key features of the dual banking system require non-deposit-receiving entities to remain within state prerogative. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]

Johnson et al. cite the DFS — and state attorneys general more broadly — as important, nimble enforcement pioneers in the privacy and algorithmic regulation space, analogous to Danielle Keats Citron's observation about state attorneys general filling gaps in privacy law. The DFS had previously led national regulators by introducing the "BitLicense" for digital currency businesses and announcing a Research and Innovation Division in 2019 to adapt consumer protections to algorithmic markets. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]

## Key facts

- Filed federal claims challenging OCC Fintech Charter Decision in *Vullo v. OCC* (S.D.N.Y., 2017), and the case was ultimately allowed to proceed after the OCC's formal announcement. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
- Introduced the "BitLicense" — one of the first regulatory regimes for digital currency businesses nationally. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
- Announced a Research and Innovation Division in July 2019 to enable the agency to adapt consumer protections in an evolving, algorithm-driven market. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
