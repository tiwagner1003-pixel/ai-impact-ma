# Frank Pasquale

**Piper & Marbury Professor of Law at the University of Maryland Francis King Carey School of Law and Affiliate Fellow at the Yale Information Society Project; scholar of algorithmic regulation, black-box AI in finance, and digital platform governance.**

## Overview

Frank Pasquale is co-author of the 2019 Fordham Law Review essay on AI, machine learning, and bias in finance. He is best known for his book "The Black Box Society: The Secret Algorithms That Control Money and Information" (Harvard UP, 2015), which provides an early and influential critique of algorithmic opacity in financial services. His prior work on "bittersweet mysteries of machine learning" (LSE Media Policy Blog, 2016) is cited in the Fordham essay. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]

The Fordham essay extends his earlier arguments: not only are financial algorithms opaque black boxes, but the regulatory architecture being constructed around fintech (specifically the OCC Fintech Charter Decision) actively dismantles the state-level oversight most capable of detecting and correcting algorithmic discrimination. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
