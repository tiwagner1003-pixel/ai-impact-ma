# Does digital transformation affect corporate mergers and acquisitions? From the perspective of information asymmetry

**Using Chinese A-share listed companies (2008–2022, N=22,274 firm-year observations), this paper demonstrates that corporate digital transformation significantly promotes M&A activity primarily by reducing external information asymmetry between acquirers and targets, while simultaneously suppressing M&A driven by managerial self-interest through reduced internal (principal-agent) information asymmetry.**

## Key takeaways

- Digital transformation significantly promotes corporate M&A behavior, including both the likelihood of engaging in deals and the volume and magnitude of transactions.
- The primary causal channel is the reduction of external information asymmetry between acquirers and target firms — not transaction cost reduction.
- The effect operates through two opposing mechanisms: (1) reducing external information asymmetry promotes M&A; (2) reducing internal information asymmetry between management and shareholders inhibits self-interested, agency-driven M&A.
- The positive effect of digital transformation on M&A is more pronounced in non-high-tech firms and firms lacking political connections — both groups that stand to gain most from improved information access.
- Regional internet development negatively moderates the relationship: digital transformation adds less marginal M&A-promoting value in areas already well-served by internet infrastructure.
- Results survive a comprehensive battery of robustness checks: Poisson/Logit models, five alternative DT measures, exclusion of exceptional samples, alternative fixed effects, Broadband China policy control, and instrumental variable regressions.

## Claims

- Digital transformation (measured as log keyword frequency of digital-related terms in annual reports) has a statistically significant positive effect on M&A scale (coeff. 0.1432, p<0.01) after controlling for firm and year fixed effects. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The positive effect holds across four alternative dependent variables: M&A transaction value (M&A_scale), transaction count (M&A_q), deal occurrence dummy (M&A), and a Poisson/Logit respecification. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Five alternative DT measures — keyword frequency in MD&A section (DT1), digital economy patents (DT2), intangible assets related to digitalization (DT3), number of executives with digital background (DT4), and CSMAR digital transformation index (DT5) — all yield positive and significant coefficients, confirming robustness. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Digital transformation significantly promotes cross-regional M&A (coeff. 0.1145, p<0.01) and cross-industry M&A (coeff. 0.1117, p<0.01), but has no significant effect on local or same-industry M&A — directly implicating external information asymmetry reduction as the mechanism. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Digital transformation promotes equity M&A (coeff. 0.1180, p<0.01) but not asset M&A — because equity acquisitions require comprehensive evaluation of a target's full financial, operational, and legal profile (higher external information demands), while asset deals have narrower information requirements. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Internal information asymmetry mechanism tests (Table 11): digital transformation significantly reduces agency costs (AC: coeff. −0.0035, p<0.05; AC2: coeff. +0.0100, p<0.05; AC3: coeff. +0.0104, p<0.05) — confirming that DT reduces principal-agent information gaps within the firm. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Digital transformation does not significantly promote M&A by high-tech companies (coeff. 0.0706, n.s.) but does significantly promote M&A by non-high-tech companies (coeff. 0.1798, p<0.01) — because high-tech firms already have superior information acquisition systems. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Digital transformation does not significantly promote M&A by politically connected firms (coeff. 0.0207, n.s.) but does significantly promote M&A by non-politically-connected firms (coeff. 0.1655, p<0.01) — because politically connected firms already have privileged information access channels. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The interaction term DT × NET (regional internet broadband subscribers) is significantly negative (coeff. −0.0152, p<0.05) — meaning higher regional internet penetration attenuates the promoting effect of digital transformation on M&A. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Instrumental variable regressions using (IV1) historical telecom data × internet broadband subscribers and (IV2) mean DT of same-region-industry peers both confirm the positive causal effect of digital transformation on M&A (second-stage coeff. 0.1981 and 0.263 respectively, p<0.01). [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Information asymmetry in M&A manifests in two dimensions: external (between acquirer and target, leading to adverse selection and moral hazard) and internal (between management and shareholders, enabling principal-agent M&A driven by managerial self-interest per Mueller 1969). [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The net overall effect of digital transformation on M&A is positive and significant — meaning external information asymmetry reduction dominates the internal channel — establishing that digital transformation "primarily reduces external information asymmetry." [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The study explicitly distinguishes its contribution from transaction cost theory (Tu & He, 2023): information asymmetry theory more directly explains cross-regional and cross-industry M&A variance than transaction cost theory does. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The Broadband China policy (national internet infrastructure initiative from 2013, 39 pilot cities 2014–2016) is controlled for as a potential confound; DT remains significant (coeff. 0.1368, p<0.01) after inclusion of the policy dummy. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Sample: Chinese A-share listed companies 2008–2022; 2306 firms; 22,274 firm-year observations; financial industry firms excluded; ST/PT companies excluded; transactions <1 million RMB excluded; 1%–99% Winsorization applied. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

## Notes

**Publication details.** Published in *Economic Analysis and Policy*, Vol. 86 (June 2025), pp. 764–778. Elsevier. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eap.2025.03.049. Received 18 June 2023; revised 16 March 2025; accepted 26 March 2025; available online 27 March 2025. Corresponding author: Shujing Yue (yueshujing@seu.edu.cn), Southeast University.

**Context for seminar paper.** This paper provides the theoretical backbone for Grundlagenteil section 2.2 of the Thema 7 seminar paper. It is the strongest empirical evidence currently in the wiki for the claim that AI/digital technologies create M&A value by reducing information asymmetry — filling the theoretical gap identified in the [[ai-in-ma]] open questions ("Are there established theoretical frameworks connecting AI capabilities to M&A value creation?").

**Key quote on dual mechanism (p. 765):** "Digital transformation can both suppress M&As by reducing internal information asymmetry and promote them by alleviating external information asymmetry. Therefore, the overall effect of digital transformation on corporate M&As depends on the net impact after the two mechanisms offset each other."

**Key quote on primary finding (conclusion, p. 776):** "Digital transformation primarily reduces external information asymmetry, enabling acquirers to gain a clearer understanding of the true conditions of target firms, thereby facilitating corporate M&As."

**Theoretical grounding.** Paper builds on Akerlof (1970) "market for lemons" (adverse selection) and Myers & Majluf (1984) (information asymmetry in financing decisions). Mueller (1969) provides the internal agency theory of conglomerate M&A. These foundational papers are cited throughout but not separately ingested.

**DT measurement methodology.** The primary DT measure is log(1 + frequency of digital-related keywords in the firm's annual report). This text-mining approach follows Chinese listed-company literature (Fang & Liu, 2024; Zeng et al., 2022) and is validated against four alternative measures with consistent results.

**Data.** CSMAR (China Stock Market & Accounting Research) database for M&A events and control variables; CNRDS (Chinese Research Data Services) for digital transformation data. Sample period 2008–2022 chosen to avoid pre/post 2007 accounting standard break and to capture post-financial-crisis Chinese M&A surge.

**Potential seminar paper use.** This paper can be cited as: (1) theoretical framework — information asymmetry as the mechanism through which digital tools affect M&A; (2) empirical evidence — DT significantly promotes M&A at the firm level; (3) moderation logic — why AI tools have heterogeneous effects (non-high-tech, non-politically-connected firms benefit most). It is peer-reviewed and published in a reputable Elsevier journal, making it preferable to practitioner sources for theoretical claims.

**Open questions.**
- Does the same dual-mechanism framework apply when "digital transformation" is narrowed to AI specifically (vs. broader digitalization)?
- The sample is Chinese A-share firms; generalizability to Western M&A contexts (different governance, different internet infrastructure) needs caution.
- The paper does not address AI quality directly — it uses digital keyword frequency as a proxy for digitalization maturity, not AI capability per se.
- How does this paper's information-asymmetry channel relate to the MASS study (Degen et al., 2024), which shows LLMs can extract managerial private M&A information from earnings calls — effectively a new form of external information asymmetry reduction?
