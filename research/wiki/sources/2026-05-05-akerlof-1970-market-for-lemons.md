# The Market for "Lemons": Quality Uncertainty and the Market Mechanism

**Akerlof (1970) is the foundational economics article showing how quality uncertainty and asymmetric information can reduce market quality, shrink market size, and create adverse selection.**

## Key takeaways

- When buyers cannot observe quality before purchase, sellers of low-quality goods have an incentive to pass them off as average-quality goods, lowering average market quality. 
- The "lemons" mechanism shows that asymmetric information can produce market failure even without irrational actors.
- Institutions such as warranties, brand names, licensing, and other quality signals arise partly to counteract information asymmetry.
- For an M&A paper, the source is useful as the theoretical foundation for why due diligence, valuation, and information-processing tools matter.

## Claims

- Markets with quality uncertainty can suffer a reduction in both average product quality and market size because private returns to high quality are not fully captured by individual sellers. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]]
- The used-car example formalizes adverse selection: if buyers price based on average expected quality, high-quality sellers may exit and low-quality sellers dominate. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]]
- Counteracting institutions can improve welfare by reducing information uncertainty, but such institutions may also create concentrations of power. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]]
