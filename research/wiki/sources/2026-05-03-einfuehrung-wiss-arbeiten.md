# Wissenschaftliches Arbeiten: Eine Einführung (SS 2026)

**Slide-deck distributed by the Goethe-Universität Frankfurt chair for Strategisches Management as the binding academic-writing guide for the SS 2026 Bachelor seminar on M&A, covering scientific method, literature research, formatting, citation style, paper structure, and grading criteria.**

## Key takeaways

- Scientific writing is defined as systematic, traceable knowledge-seeking; all claims must be backed by verifiable sources — opinions and unsubstantiated statements disqualify work as non-scientific.
- AI tools (LLMs) are explicitly not scientific sources; they may assist with orientation and search-term development, but every AI-generated claim must be independently verified against peer-reviewed literature.
- The seminar uses Harvard in-text citation style: `(Autor Jahr, Seitenangabe)` in parentheses; plagiarism automatically results in a "mangelhaft" (failing) grade.
- AI usage must be transparently documented in a dedicated AI-tools index (separate from the bibliography), listing tool name, version, mode of use, and affected sections of the paper.
- Paper structure follows a fixed sequence: Einleitung → Grundlagenteil → Hauptteil → Schlussteil → Literaturverzeichnis; the Gliederung (outline) should be developed before writing begins.
- Formatting is tightly prescribed: 25–30 text pages, Arial or Times New Roman 12 pt, 1.5-line spacing in text / single spacing in footnotes, block justification, and specific page margins.
- Grading is multi-dimensional: treatment of the research question, logical consistency of results, definitional precision, intellectual independence, language/style, structure, and formal compliance.

## Claims

- Scientific work (Wissenschaftliches Arbeiten) is defined as the systematic and traceable pursuit of knowledge (systematische und nachvollziehbare Erkenntnissuche).
- Non-scientific writing relies on personal opinions, unsubstantiated claims ("man weiß", "es ist offensichtlich"), and selective or unsystematic source selection.
- The quality of the literature research directly determines the quality of the paper (Qualität der Recherche bestimmt die Qualität der Arbeit).
- AI (KI) is not a scientific source in the classical sense; AI-generated content must be critically verified; AI can generate incorrect or untraceable content.
- Literature research should combine database search (JSTOR, WISO, EconLit, Web of Science, Scopus) with the snowball method (Schneeballsystem); no single database is sufficient.
- The Gliederung (outline) is the most important key to the quality of the paper and should be created before writing begins.
- Each section of the paper must cover exactly one content area; sections should be at minimum 0.5 pages and no more than 2–3 pages long.
- Page layout: left margin 4 cm, right margin 2 cm, top margin 2.5 cm, bottom margin 3 cm; these margins apply to body text, footnotes, lists, and appendix alike.
- Paper length: 25–30 Textseiten including figures; font Arial or Times New Roman 12 pt body / 10 pt footnotes; line spacing 1.5 in text, single in footnotes; block justification with automatic hyphenation.
- Citation form used at the chair is Harvard style: in-text short reference as `(Author Year, page)`, with full reference in the bibliography.
- Direct quotations are marked with quotation marks and must cite the source; indirect (paraphrase) citations precede the reference with "vgl.".
- Plagiarism (Verstoß gegen die Zitierpflicht) inevitably leads to a grade of "mangelhaft" (insufficient/failing).
- Lecture slides (Vorlesungsskripte) are not citable as scientific sources.
- AI-generated content, visualizations, data transformations, and idea-finding uses must each be disclosed — in-text or in footnotes — and recorded in a separate AI-tools index (KI-Verzeichnis) beyond the standard bibliography.
- Tools exempt from the KI-Verzeichnis requirement include Microsoft Office, LaTeX, Google Scholar, university library databases, reference managers (Zotero, Endnote, Mendeley, Citavi), spell/grammar checkers, online dictionaries, and statistical software (R, Python, SPSS).
- Grading criteria span seven dimensions: (1) treatment of the research question, (2) logical consistency of results, (3) definitional precision and consistent use of terms, (4) intellectual independence and engagement with literature gaps, (5) language and style, (6) structure and presentation, (7) formal requirements including citation compliance.
- The Einleitung must not contain definitions, tables, figures, or detailed results; it answers "Worum geht es und warum ist das wichtig?"
- The Grundlagenteil provides theoretical context and defines central terms; a common error is providing a pure summary without connection to the research question.
- The Hauptteil delivers structured analysis, systematic argumentation, and critical engagement with the literature; pure description instead of analysis is a typical error.
- The Schlussteil summarizes results, ties back to the research question, and ends with the paper's core statement (Kernaussage); introducing new content (other than an Ausblick) is an error.
- Quality arises from clarity and structure, not from length (Qualität entsteht durch Klarheit und Struktur, nicht durch Umfang).

## Notes

Key quote on AI and citations: "KI ist keine wissenschaftliche Quelle im klassischen Sinne!" and "KI erfindet oft Literaturangaben" — directly relevant to Thema 7 because the paper will discuss AI yet cannot cite AI outputs as evidence.

The AI-tools index requirement is distinctive: a full table (KI-basiertes Hilfsmittel + Version | Einsatzform | Betroffene Teile der Arbeit | Bemerkungen) is mandatory whenever any AI tool is used for substantive tasks. ChatGPT 3.5 is given as the example in the slide.

Reference to "Handlungsempfehlungen der Goethe-Universität zum Einsatz generativer KI" — this appears to be a separate institutional policy document worth ingesting.

The prescribed writing order is counter-intuitive: Grundlagenteil (1) → Hauptteil (2) → Einleitung (3) → Schlussteil (4), not the reading order. This is worth flagging for the paper-writing workflow.

Heading formulation tip: explain the section's content to an imaginary person, then condense that explanation into a noun-phrase heading (no complete sentences, no punctuation, no formulas or abbreviations).

Snowball method caveat: tends to miss uncited and recent work; must be supplemented by systematic database searches.
