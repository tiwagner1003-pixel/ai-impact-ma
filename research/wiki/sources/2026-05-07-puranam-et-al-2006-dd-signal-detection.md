# Due diligence failure as a signal detection problem

**Strategic Organization article modelling DD as decision-making under uncertainty: acquirers must decide whether negative but noisy DD findings are true warning signals or noise.**

## Key takeaways

- The paper reframes DD quality as a signal-detection problem rather than simply a search problem: even when negative information is found, acquirers may fail by not updating valuation or deal decisions appropriately.
- DD findings are usually negative and noisy because targets have incentives to disclose favourable information publicly while unfavourable information is revealed privately during DD.
- Acquirers face two error types: walking away from a value-enhancing acquisition because DD signals are false alarms, or continuing with a value-destroying acquisition because true warning signals are ignored.
- An experimental DD simulation shows that initial valuation affects how negative DD information changes valuations and final acquisition decisions.
- Nearly 36% of subjects proceeded to make binding offers even when negative DD information, taken at face value, should have reduced valuations about 30% below bid values.
- The paper is exceptionally useful for the seminar's "quality" dimension: AI can improve detection/recall, but DD quality also depends on how humans interpret and act on signals.

## Claims

- Relevant information uncovered during DD is usually unfavourable and lowers the target's value to the acquirer. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- DD failure occurs either when acquirers fail to discover negative information or when they discover it but fail to respond appropriately by withdrawing or revising bids. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- Due diligence is often the acquirer's last chance to walk away from a value-destroying acquisition. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- Signal detection theory fits DD because acquirers must decide whether negative information is a true signal of overvaluation or noise. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- The cost trade-off between Type I and Type II errors shapes how seriously acquirers take DD findings. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- The study found support for a signal-detection perspective on decision persistence in DD, distinct from a pure escalation-of-commitment explanation. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- Decisions that look like DD failures ex post may have been defensible ex ante under uncertainty, depending on the decision maker's payoff matrix. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
