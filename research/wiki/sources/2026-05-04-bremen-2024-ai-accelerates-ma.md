# AI accelerates M&A into the future

**A practitioner article by WTW Senior Managing Director John M. Bremen arguing that effective leaders are already using AI across all M&A deal stages to streamline processes, and outlining six concrete action areas for responsible AI adoption in M&A.**

## Key takeaways

- Generative AI adoption in M&A deal processes is currently at only 16% prevalence (BCG survey, 2024), but expert practitioners predict this will rise to 80% within three years — making now the window for building AI capabilities.
- AI is applicable across the full M&A lifecycle: sell-side preparation, buy-side target identification and valuation, due diligence, and integration management.
- Responsible AI governance in M&A requires actively addressing confidentiality, bias, regulatory/liability diversity, and ethical usage — not just productivity efficiency gains.
- AI is expected to produce job-role shifts toward higher-value human activities (relationship management, negotiation, strategic decision making) rather than wholesale job elimination.
- Prompt engineering is specifically cited as a practitioner skill for risk detection and pattern identification in M&A diligence contexts.
- Effective leaders do not wait for AI disruption to arrive — they invest proactively in AI skills training and data safeguards.

## Claims

- According to a BCG survey (2024), generative AI is used in M&A deal processes at a 16% prevalence rate as of mid-2024.
- Expert M&A practitioners predict generative AI adoption in deal processes will reach approximately 80% within three years of 2024.
- AI applications span all M&A stages: assisting sell-side bankers preparing assets for sale, buy-side target identification and valuation, due diligence document review, data analysis and risk assessment, and integration management.
- AI automates document review, data analysis, and risk assessment in M&A, freeing practitioners to focus on strategic decision making and creative problem solving.
- Effective M&A practitioners use AI-powered deal tracking tools to automate responses to common deal queries, manage multiple workstreams, and reduce errors and delays.
- Practitioners use AI prompt engineering in M&A diligence to enhance efficiency, focus on key risks, detect patterns or discrepancies, and identify risk-reduction opportunities.
- A responsible AI governance framework for M&A includes six components: (1) understanding how AI tools work and their limitations; (2) educating users on benefits and risks; (3) establishing ethical usage standards; (4) keeping confidential deal data safe from AI training exposure; (5) addressing algorithmic bias in data inputs and outputs; (6) understanding jurisdiction-specific regulatory and liability rules.
- An Evercore ISI / David Shrier report concludes that almost every job will be impacted by AI in some way, but only partially in most cases — AI is primarily a productivity enhancer and job-role shifter rather than a job eliminator.
- In M&A, AI replacing repetitive and mundane tasks allows dealmakers to focus on higher-value human activities: relationship management, negotiation, and strategic decision making.
- The most ubiquitous AI tools — LLMs such as ChatGPT — enable M&A practitioners to integrate AI into everyday deal tasks without specialist infrastructure.
- Effective leaders began investing in AI, implementing skills training and data safeguards immediately when generative AI proliferated — proactive adoption provides a competitive advantage.
- Confidentiality risk in AI adoption includes preventing proprietary deal information from being exposed to public AI learning and training systems; legal and security reviews of AI services are required.
- Understanding the competitive landscape of dedicated AI-powered M&A tools and platforms is identified as a distinct responsibility for M&A leaders.

## Notes

- This article is cited as "Bremen (2024)" in the Goethe-Universitat Frankfurt SS 2026 seminar syllabus (Thema 7 prescribed reading). It originally appeared in Forbes on May 31, 2024; the WTW website republished it on June 26, 2024.
- The article is practitioner-facing, not peer-reviewed. It draws on an M&A roundtable facilitated by WTW with Fortune 500 organizations, and on a BCG survey. No primary data or methodology is reported; claims should be treated as practitioner opinion backed by survey data.
- The BCG 16%→80% projection is attributed to a "recent BCG survey" without further bibliographic detail — this should not be cited as a primary empirical finding in the seminar paper without identifying the original BCG survey.
- John M. Bremen is identified as Senior Managing Director and Chief Innovation and Acceleration Officer at WTW (Willis Towers Watson). WTW is a global advisory, broking, and solutions company — it has a direct commercial interest in M&A advisory, which should be noted when assessing the article's framing.
- The article is structured as a leadership advice piece ("effective leaders do X") rather than a scholarly argument. The six action areas are normative prescriptions, not empirical findings.
- The Evercore ISI / David Shrier citation is unspecified (no publication title, date, or URL) — should be tracked down if cited in the seminar paper.
- Key quote: "When it comes to AI in M&A, effective leaders know they don't need to go first, but they know they shouldn't go last."
- Open question: The article predicts 80% AI adoption within three years — does subsequent data (post-2024) confirm, accelerate, or revise this trajectory?
- This source complements the three academic/empirical Thema 7 readings: it provides the practitioner framing and adoption-trajectory data that contextualizes why the academic research (Degen et al., Zhang et al., Singh) matters in practice.
