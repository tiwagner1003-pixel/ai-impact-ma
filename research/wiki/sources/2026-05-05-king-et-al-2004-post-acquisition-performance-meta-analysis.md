# Meta-analyses of Post-acquisition Performance

**King et al. (2004) meta-analyze acquisition-performance research and find that acquisitions do not, on average, improve acquirer performance; unexplained moderators remain central.**

## Key takeaways

- The study synthesizes 93 published studies on post-acquisition performance.
- Acquiring firms' performance does not positively change on average as a function of acquisition activity and is modestly negatively affected.
- Commonly studied antecedents do not consistently explain post-acquisition performance.
- The authors argue that unidentified moderators and better theory are needed to explain why some acquisitions succeed while many do not.

## Claims

- Across the commonly studied variables, acquiring-firm performance does not reliably improve after acquisitions. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
- Existing empirical research has not consistently identified antecedents that predict post-acquisition performance. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
- The results imply a need for additional theory development and changes in M&A research methods. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
