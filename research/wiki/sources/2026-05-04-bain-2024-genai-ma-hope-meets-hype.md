# Generative AI in M&A: Where Hope Meets Hype

**Bain & Company practitioner article (January 2024) reporting results of a survey of 300+ M&A practitioners on generative AI adoption: 16% current usage, 80% expected within three years, 85% satisfaction rate among early adopters, and a framework of three strategic questions for getting started.**

## Key takeaways

- Generative AI adoption in M&A deal processes stands at only 16% today, but 80% of surveyed practitioners expect to use it within the next three years — representing rapid near-term growth expectations from this Bain survey of 300+ practitioners.
- Current adopters concentrate GenAI usage in the earliest M&A phases — target sourcing and screening (38–50% of users) and conducting due diligence (58%) — with integration planning (22%) and execution (10%) seeing far less usage.
- 85% of current generative AI users in M&A report the technology met or exceeded their expectations; 78% cited reduced manual effort and 54% cited accelerated timelines as primary benefits.
- The top concerns cited by non-users are data inaccuracy (59%), data privacy (38%), and cybersecurity risk (36%), forming a clear risk hierarchy that practitioners must address before wider adoption.
- Efficiency gains from GenAI do not automatically translate into better deals; the article warns that reviewing more deals faster is not equivalent to making better decisions, and competitive differentiation requires building on existing M&A process strengths.
- Long-term competitive advantage requires migrating from generic third-party GenAI tools toward proprietary data-enriched models — frequent acquirers already hold sizeable internal datasets that could feed such tools.

## Claims

- Only 16% of M&A practitioners surveyed were deploying generative AI at the time of the survey (early 2024); 16% of non-users said they were likely to adopt within the next 12 months. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- 80% of respondents expected to use generative AI in their M&A processes within three years. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Early adopters are primarily in technology, healthcare, and finance sectors, and tend to be larger companies with moderate M&A activity (three to five deals per year). [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Current generative AI usage in M&A is concentrated in the "finding the right deal" phase: developing M&A strategy (38% of current users), sourcing targets (50%), and screening targets (50%). [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- During the "validating the deal" phase, 58% of current users apply GenAI to conducting due diligence, compared to only 22% for integration planning. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- In the "delivering the value" phase, just 10% of current users apply GenAI to integration execution — the phase with the lowest current adoption. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- 85% of early generative AI users in M&A reported that the technology met or exceeded their expectations. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Among early users, 78% cited reduced manual effort, 54% cited accelerated timelines, 42% cited reduced cost, and 42% cited improved focus as key benefits. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- The top three risks cited by non-users were data inaccuracy (59%), data privacy risk (38%), and cybersecurity risk (36%). [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Survey data are from the "Bain M&A Practitioners' 2024 Outlook Survey"; current-users sample is N=50; non-users sample is N=256; the total base is approximately 306 respondents. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Generative AI can pick up acquisition targets not identifiable with traditional screening tools, according to one practitioner quoted in the article. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- GenAI can be trained to parse material contracts and identify deviations from a model contract, saving time and directing reviewers toward problematic areas. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Being more efficient through GenAI means a firm can look at more deals, but does not guarantee better deal decisions — it is what practitioners do with freed-up time that makes a difference. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Third-party vendor GenAI tools without proprietary data or models are sufficient for most firms today, but building a sustainable competitive edge ultimately requires proprietary data enrichment. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Generative AI cannot replace a skilled M&A practitioner in the driver's seat; best-in-class acquirers who have perfected the fundamentals of dealmaking will consistently outperform less experienced peers regardless of AI use. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]

## Notes

**Survey attribution note (important for seminar paper).** Bremen (2024) — the prescribed Thema 7 reading already in the wiki — cites the 16%/80% GenAI adoption figures and attributes them to a "BCG survey." This Bain article is the direct source of those same figures, published under "Bain M&A Practitioners' 2024 Outlook Survey" (N~306). The Bremen article may have confused or mis-attributed the source, or may be citing a separate BCG survey with coincidentally similar results. A conflict callout has been inserted on the [[generative-ai-in-ma]] and [[boston-consulting-group]] pages. **For the seminar paper, cite this Bain article directly for the 16%/80% figures, not Bremen (2024) as an intermediary.**

**Practitioner framing vs. academic rigour.** This article is practitioner thought-leadership from Bain & Company, published in January 2024 as part of Bain's annual M&A Report. It is not peer-reviewed. The survey methodology is described only briefly (N≈306, figures from charts include N=50 current users and N=256 non-users). No confidence intervals or sampling methodology details are provided. The article is useful for documenting practitioner expectations and benefit/risk perceptions, but empirical claims need corroboration from peer-reviewed sources.

**Quote worth preserving (practitioner voice on screening):** "Generative AI in the screening process can pick up targets that would not be identified with traditional tools."

**Quote worth preserving (practitioner voice on diligence):** "Generative AI is helpful in parsing the mountain of data that needs to be reviewed. If you miss a critical fact, it can be a loss. Generative AI can be trained to parse material contracts and identify deviations from a model contract, saving time and helping to focus on problematic areas."

**Quote worth preserving (challenge — review overhead):** "In terms of realizing benefits, it takes us as much time to go through generative AI as it saves us in writing summaries or crafting reports."

**Quote worth preserving (limitation — public data):** "It's not an issue in idea generation during screening, but it is a challenge in steps like valuing deals" — and targets are unlikely to allow potential acquirers access to internal data through GenAI tools.

**Open question surfaced.** Bain's three strategic questions for GenAI adoption (where is value highest, where can differentiation be built, how to mitigate risks) are a practitioner framework that could serve as a structuring device in the seminar paper's recommendations section.

**Related reading.** The same 2024 Bain M&A Report includes a chapter on regulatory scrutiny ("Regulation and M&A: How Scrutiny Raises the Bar for Acquirers") — potentially useful for Thema 2/5 if a broader literature review is required.
