# Corporate Financing and Investment Decisions When Firms Have Information That Investors Do Not Have

**Myers & Majluf (1984) extend information asymmetry into corporate finance by showing how managers' private information affects financing and investment decisions, including reluctance to issue equity.**

## Key takeaways

- The model assumes managers know more about firm value than outside investors.
- Under asymmetric information, issuing equity can signal that management believes shares are overvalued.
- Firms may reject positive-NPV investment opportunities if financing them through undervalued equity would harm existing shareholders.
- The paper supports the pecking-order intuition: firms prefer internal funds, then debt, and are reluctant to issue equity.

## Claims

- Managerial private information can make financing decisions informative signals to outside investors. [[2026-05-05-myers-majluf-1984-corporate-financing-investment-decisions]]
- Asymmetric information can lead firms to pass up valuable investment projects when external equity financing would transfer value from existing to new shareholders. [[2026-05-05-myers-majluf-1984-corporate-financing-investment-decisions]]
- The model helps explain why firms rely on internal funds and prefer debt to equity when external financing is needed. [[2026-05-05-myers-majluf-1984-corporate-financing-investment-decisions]]
