# The Transformative Potential of Artificial Intelligence and its Impact on M&A Transactions

**A legal-practitioner survey of how AI technologies — including machine learning and deep learning — can improve each phase of the M&A transaction lifecycle while arguing that human judgment remains essential and can never be fully replaced.**

## Key takeaways

- AI can accelerate and improve every phase of the M&A lifecycle (pre-merger target screening, merger-phase due diligence, post-merger integration and compliance), but human judgment remains irreplaceable in negotiations and interpersonal deal dynamics.
- Over 50% of M&A transactions fail to produce expected results; traditional manual processes are slow, expensive, and prone to overlooking critical signals, motivating the adoption of AI.
- In the merger (due diligence) phase, AI simultaneously analyses financial, legal, tax, HR, IT, and environmental data from target organisations, reducing human error and enabling more precise risk assessment.
- In the pre-merger phase, AI-driven SWOT analyses and automated "company profiling" screen candidates against predefined criteria, cutting time and resource costs while allowing managers to focus on high-promise targets.
- Post-merger, AI expert systems can conduct financial audits, pinpoint compliance decision paths, and simplify contract management — but cultural integration and organisational change management remain fundamentally human tasks.
- Machine learning, deep learning, and traditional AI are distinguished by capability level: deep learning uses multi-layer neural networks to handle unstructured, high-dimensional data and is specifically positioned as the technology that can "change the cycle" of M&A transactions.
- The lean methodology (originating in automotive manufacturing) is presented as a complementary process framework alongside AI to address the cost and efficiency pitfalls of the conventional M&A approach.

## Claims

- More than 50% of M&A transactions fail to produce the expected results, representing a significant failure rate tied to both hard problems (structuring, financing) and soft "human factors" (culture, communication, teamwork).
- Traditional M&A methodology covers every phase from pre-deal to post-closing but has not evolved for decades, is demanding, time-consuming, and expensive, and is frequently criticised for this stagnation.
- In the context of M&A, AI has at least six distinct application areas: (1) contract and due diligence review, (2) data analysis and predictive analytics, (3) target identification, (4) market intelligence, (5) automated compliance and regulatory analysis, and (6) expedient post-merger integration.
- AI-powered software can rapidly analyse large volumes of contracts and legal papers, spotting important clauses, potential pitfalls, and business prospects, thereby lowering the possibility of human error in due diligence.
- AI algorithms can process data from multiple sources to offer market analysis, risk assessment, and transaction appraisal, and can recognise patterns and trends to improve decision-making during the negotiation phase.
- AI-powered target identification solutions can filter large datasets by predefined acquirer criteria and link businesses with similar strategic objectives, automating initial candidate screening.
- AI can help detect and navigate complicated regulatory requirements, guaranteeing compliance with laws that may influence the M&A deal.
- In the post-merger phase, AI can examine data to verify that the seller adheres to promised accounting and valuation standards, and AI expert systems can pinpoint crucial decision paths to make post-deal due diligence more effective and risk-free.
- During the merger phase, AI's function in negotiations and contract agreements is constrained because human relationships are complicated and dynamic — tactical judgments and interpersonal relationships are vital in this stage.
- Deep learning models, which use multi-layered neural networks, can automatically create hierarchical representations from raw data, making them effective at managing unstructured and high-dimensional data; such models are "trainable technologies" that improve when exposed to different types of transactions.
- The lean technique, imported from manufacturing, applies a "just in time" strategy to M&A due diligence, reducing unnecessary resource use and costs by mapping value flows and iterating cyclically until the process aligns with client needs.
- At a World Economic Forum panel, Ginni Rometty and Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella highlighted that AI complements rather than replaces human capabilities — a position the paper uses to frame AI as a decision-support aid, not a substitute for human M&A expertise.
- The M&A process in India is formulated in five stages: (1) M&A strategy formulation, (2) target identification and initial assessment, (3) target valuation and due diligence, (4) deal structure negotiation and closing, (5) post-merger integration.
- AI has the ability to significantly improve M&A deals by enhancing efficiency and decision-making, but its limitations must be acknowledged and human expertise must be matched with AI skills for desired results.

## Notes

**Citation note:** The paper was received 25 July 2023, accepted 12 August 2023, and published 16 August 2023 in Jus Corpus Law Journal, Vol. 3, Issue 4 (June–August 2023), pp. 249–260. The Bluebook citation places it as "3 Jus Corpus L.J. 249 (2022)" — the "2022" in the Bluebook citation appears to reference the journal volume year, while the actual publication date is 2023. The syllabus entry for this paper lists it as "Singh (2022)" following the Bluebook convention. Use **Singh (2023)** when citing by actual publication date, or follow the syllabus's "Singh (2022)" when referring to the prescribed reading list entry.

**Scope:** This is a legal survey paper, not an empirical study. It synthesises existing knowledge about AI applications in M&A from a legal-practitioner perspective. It does not present original data, model benchmarks, or quantitative findings. Its value as a seminar source is as an overview of the AI application landscape and a legal/regulatory framing of AI in M&A deals.

**Prescribed reading:** This paper is one of the four prescribed starting readings for Thema 7 of the SS 2026 Goethe-Universitat Frankfurt Bachelor seminar.

**Key limitation:** The paper's empirical claims about M&A failure rates (>50%) and AI capabilities are supported primarily by practitioner blogs, news articles, and secondary sources (Thomson Reuters, Datasaur, Baker Donelson, Firmex, IBM), rather than peer-reviewed academic literature. The Zhang et al. (2024) and Degen et al. (2024) papers provide stronger empirical grounding for specific AI capability claims.

**Quotes worth preserving:**
- "AI has the ability to significantly improve M&A deals by enhancing efficiency and decision-making. However, in order to achieve the desired results, its limitations should be acknowledged, and human expertise should be matched with AI skills."
- "The article underlines the significance of adopting AI technology to maintain competitiveness and adjust to the changing M&A transaction landscape."
- On deep learning in M&A: "The same in an M&A transaction will successfully handle the tiresome procedure by changing the cycle into an effective, outcome-driven method."

**Open questions raised:**
- What does AI-assisted post-merger cultural integration look like concretely? The paper asserts AI can help manage cultural differences but provides no case studies or mechanisms.
- The lean methodology discussion is underdeveloped in relation to AI — is there empirical evidence that lean + AI outperforms either approach alone?
- The paper was published in an open-access Indian law journal (CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0); what is the academic standing of Jus Corpus Law Journal in the M&A / finance literature?
