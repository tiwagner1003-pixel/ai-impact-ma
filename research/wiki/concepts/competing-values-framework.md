# Competing Values Framework (CVF)

**A widely used organizational culture typology (Cameron et al. 2006; Quinn and Rohrbaugh 1983) that conceptualizes culture along four dimensions — Adhocracy, Clan, Market, and Hierarchy — arranged across two axes (flexibility vs. control; internal vs. external focus).**

## Summary

The Competing Values Framework was developed by Quinn and Rohrbaugh (1983) and refined by Cameron et al. (2006). It organizes organizational culture into four quadrants:

- **Adhocracy (Create):** Emphasizes adaptability, flexibility, and innovation. Associated with creativity, entrepreneurship, and agility. The CVF associates adhocracy culture with creativity and generation of new ideas.
- **Clan (Collaborate):** Emphasizes collaboration, teamwork, and employee development. Associated with cohesion, participation, and morale.
- **Market (Compete):** Emphasizes task completion, goal achievement, and profitability. Associated with competitiveness, customer focus, and market share maximization.
- **Hierarchy (Control):** Emphasizes clear rules, explicit instructions, and strict controls. Associated with formal procedures, stability, and efficiency.

The four dimensions are arranged along two continuums: the organization's preference for flexibility vs. control, and its internal vs. external focus. A firm's culture is typically not exclusive to one quadrant — scores across all four dimensions sum to reflect the relative emphasis, and dominant culture is the dimension with the highest probability score. [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]

In M&A research, the CVF provides a structured vocabulary for operationalizing organizational cultural distance. Brede et al. (2025) apply Culture-BERT to classify Glassdoor employee reviews along all four CVF dimensions, then compute organizational cultural distance using the Kogut and Singh (1988) composite index applied to the four-dimensional CVF scores. [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]

## Key claims across sources

- In 57% of the 243 M&A deals in Brede et al. (2025), the dominant CVF culture (highest probability score) differed between acquirer and target. [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]
- Market culture is the most common dominant dimension for both acquirers (34% of firms) and targets (41% of firms) in the Brede et al. (2025) sample. [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]
- Performance and acquisition premium effects in M&A are primarily driven by Market dimension differences; innovation effects are split — adhocracy drives patent growth differences, hierarchy drives new product development differences. [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]
- Büschgens et al. (2013) meta-analysis finds a significant positive relationship between adhocracy culture and innovation capability, while hierarchy culture has a significant negative relationship with innovation capability. [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]
- Culture-BERT achieves up to 28% higher accuracy on CVF classification than word2vec-based methods (Koch and Pasch 2022). [[2026-05-04-brede-et-al-2025-cultural-distance-ma-glassdoor]]
