# Moral Hazard

**The ex post problem arising when a party to a transaction exploits an information advantage after contracting to act in ways the other party cannot observe and would not sanction.**

## Summary

Moral hazard is the ex post counterpart to adverse selection. Whereas adverse selection concerns hidden information before a contract is signed, moral hazard concerns hidden actions or changed behavior after commitment. In agency theory terms, once the principal has hired or contracted with the agent, the agent may shirk, misreport, or pursue self-interested strategies that the principal cannot easily detect or verify. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

In management research, moral hazard is framed as "hidden information leads to pre- or postcontractual opportunism" — the third conceptualization of information asymmetry identified by Bergh et al. (2019). "Given bounded rationality and information asymmetries, it may be impossible for a principal to specify performance criteria ex ante and to contract for the services of the best possible agent. Thus, relational or long-term contracting alleviates moral hazard problems stemming from divergence between the interests of principal and agent." (Gomez-Mejia & Balkin 1992, quoted in Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

In M&A, moral hazard appears in multiple phases: target management may withhold unfavorable information during due diligence (pre-close), and post-acquisition the target's management may underperform or redirect resources once the deal is done (post-close). Monitoring systems, incentive alignment (earnouts, equity grants), and post-merger integration governance are the primary institutional responses. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

## Variations / sub-concepts

- Hidden action (behavioral moral hazard) — agent changes effort or conduct post-contract
- Hidden information (informational moral hazard) — agent withholds relevant post-contract information
- [[adverse-selection]] — the ex ante complement
- Shirking, misreporting, empire-building — specific manifestations in managerial contexts

## Key claims across sources

- Moral hazard is the ex post problem where information asymmetries and incentives combine to pose a hazard to agents, reducible by monitoring conduct, gaining access to internal information flows, and providing incentives encouraging agents to act in owners' best interests (Schulze et al. 2001, per Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Monitoring effectiveness is limited when an agent has high autonomy, independence, and specialized information; principals rely on incentive systems to align preferences (Gomez-Mejia & Balkin 1992, per Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Outcome-based incentives reduce moral hazard by aligning principal and agent rewards so that conflicts of self-interest are reduced (Eisenhardt 1989, per Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
