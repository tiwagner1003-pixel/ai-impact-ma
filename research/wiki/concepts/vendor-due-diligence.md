# Vendor Due Diligence

**A due diligence report commissioned and paid for by the seller (vendor) of a business, made available to prospective buyers, as an alternative or supplement to buyer-led due diligence.**

## Summary

In a standard M&A process the buyer commissions and controls its own DD investigation. Vendor due diligence (VDD) inverts this: the seller engages an adviser — typically an accounting or commercial DD firm — to produce a report that is then provided to prospective buyers, often in an auction process. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]

Howson (2003) flags a fundamental credibility risk with VDD: because the seller controls the mandate and the framing, the tone and emphasis of the report can be shaped — deliberately or through adviser incentive alignment — to present the target in the most favourable light. Buyers relying solely on VDD without supplementary own-side investigation are therefore exposed to information that is incomplete or selectively presented. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]

VDD is nonetheless commercially useful in competitive auction processes where the seller wants to give all bidders consistent information quickly, reduce management disruption from multiple parallel DD teams, and accelerate closing. These efficiency benefits are real but do not eliminate the fundamental principal-agent problem inherent in seller-commissioned diligence.

## Key claims across sources

- Vendor due diligence is commissioned by sellers; buyers should be wary because the tone and framing of VDD reports can be manipulated in the seller's favour. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- VDD introduces a structural principal-agent problem: the adviser producing the report is engaged and paid by the party with the strongest interest in a high price, not by the party bearing the risk of an inaccurate report.
