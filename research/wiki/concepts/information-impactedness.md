# Information Impactedness

**Williamson's (1975) concept describing the condition in which relevant transactional information is known to one or more parties but cannot be costlessly disclosed or verified by others, thereby raising transaction costs and enabling opportunism.**

## Summary

Information impactedness is the fifth and most transaction-cost-specific conceptualization of information asymmetry identified in management research. Williamson (1975: 40) developed the concept within his "organizational failures" framework to explain why markets sometimes fail and hierarchy (firm internalization) is preferred: "information asymmetries between potential transaction partners raises the search, monitoring, and bonding costs of a transaction due to the high cost of achieving informational parity and the proclivity to behave opportunistically when given the chance." [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

The concept differs from simpler notions of "private information" or "hidden information" by emphasizing the cost dimension: it is not merely that one party lacks information, but that achieving parity is prohibitively expensive. This expense creates a structural asymmetry that honest firms cannot easily overcome, potentially leading them to exit markets or relationships — the lemons dynamic at the market-design level. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

In M&A research, information impactedness explains why acquisitions (hierarchical governance) may be preferred over alliances when the target holds valuable, non-transferable private knowledge: acquiring the firm internalizes the information advantage rather than trying to negotiate disclosure across an information barrier. Conversely, when information impactedness is moderate, alliances can economize on asymmetry-based transaction costs by limiting specialized investment and withdrawal costs. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

AI-assisted due diligence tools are, from a TCE perspective, mechanisms for reducing information impactedness by automating the costly process of achieving informational parity — analyzing large document sets, screening contracts, and extracting structured risk signals at lower cost than traditional manual review.

## Variations / sub-concepts

- Search costs — costs of locating and evaluating transaction partners under impactedness
- Monitoring costs — costs of verifying behavior post-contract
- Bonding costs — costs borne by agents to credibly commit to non-opportunistic behavior
- [[transaction-cost-economics]] — the parent theoretical framework

## Key claims across sources

- Information impactedness leads to "the search, monitoring, and bonding costs of a transaction due to the high cost of achieving informational parity and the proclivity to behave opportunistically" (Williamson 1975, per Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- When informational parity is costly, some firms may exploit superior information to their own advantage, leading honest firms to leave the market. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Alliances reduce information asymmetry-based transaction costs while maximizing transaction value relative to full hierarchical governance in some conditions (Reuer & Koza 2000, per Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
