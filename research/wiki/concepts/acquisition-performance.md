# Acquisition Performance

**The financial, strategic, and organizational outcomes of an acquisition for the acquirer, target, combined firm, and other stakeholders.**

## Summary

King et al. (2004) find that acquisition activity does not, on average, positively change acquiring-firm performance and may modestly harm it. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]

Haleblian et al. (2009) show that M&A performance research is fragmented across finance and management, with target shareholders often gaining while acquirers experience neutral or negative returns. [[2026-05-05-haleblian-et-al-2009-ma-review]]

Graebner et al. (2017) shift attention to post-deal implementation, arguing that PMI is central to explaining M&A outcomes because integration unfolds as a process involving strategic, sociocultural, and emotional dynamics. [[2026-05-05-graebner-et-al-2017-post-merger-integration-review]]


## Key claims across sources

- Average acquirer performance after acquisitions is weak, mixed, or modestly negative. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
- Target shareholders usually benefit from acquisition premiums. [[2026-05-05-haleblian-et-al-2009-ma-review]]
- Unidentified moderators remain important for explaining why some deals succeed and others fail. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
