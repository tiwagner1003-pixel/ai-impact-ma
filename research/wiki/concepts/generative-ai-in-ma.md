# Generative AI in M&A

**The application of generative AI technologies — including large language models such as ChatGPT — to M&A deal processes, covering document generation, data analysis, risk assessment, query automation, and deal tracking.**

## Summary

Generative AI represents a specific and currently fast-growing sub-category of AI in M&A. Unlike classical machine learning models (which predict outcomes from structured data), generative AI models create text, summaries, and structured outputs from unstructured inputs — making them immediately applicable to document-heavy M&A workflows such as due diligence review, CIM drafting, and contract analysis. [[2026-05-04-bremen-2024-ai-accelerates-ma]]

Generative AI adoption in M&A deal processes stood at 16% in early 2024, with 80% of practitioners expecting to use it within three years. These figures originate in the Bain M&A Practitioners' 2024 Outlook Survey (N≈306). [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]

The practitioner framing distinguishes generative AI from broader AI/ML: LLMs such as ChatGPT are identified as the most accessible entry point for M&A practitioners because they can be integrated into everyday tasks without specialist data science infrastructure. Classical ML approaches (gradient boosting, SVM, MLP) for target selection and synergy prediction — as in Zhang et al. (2024) — require more specialized deployment. ## Key claims across sources

- Generative AI adoption in M&A deal processes is at 16% prevalence as of early 2024 (Bain survey, N≈306), with 80% of practitioners forecasting adoption within three years. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- The most ubiquitous generative AI tools — LLMs such as ChatGPT — allow M&A practitioners to incorporate AI into everyday tasks without specialist infrastructure. [[2026-05-04-bremen-2024-ai-accelerates-ma]]
- Generative AI automates document review, data analysis, and risk assessment in M&A, freeing practitioners to focus on strategic decision making and creative problem solving. [[2026-05-04-bremen-2024-ai-accelerates-ma]]
- GenAI usage is most prevalent in the early deal phases: sourcing/screening targets (38–50% of current users) and due diligence (58%), falling sharply to 22% for integration planning and 10% for integration execution. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- 85% of current GenAI users in M&A reported the technology met or exceeded expectations; 78% cited reduced manual effort and 54% cited accelerated timelines as key benefits. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- The top risks cited by non-users are data inaccuracy (59%), data privacy (38%), and cybersecurity (36%). [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Early adopters of GenAI in M&A are concentrated in technology, healthcare, and finance, and tend to be larger companies with three to five deals per year. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Efficiency gains from GenAI do not automatically translate into better deals; competitive differentiation requires building on existing M&A process strengths, not just reviewing more deals faster. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Generative AI cannot replace a skilled M&A practitioner; best-in-class acquirers who have perfected dealmaking fundamentals will consistently outperform less experienced peers regardless of AI use. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
