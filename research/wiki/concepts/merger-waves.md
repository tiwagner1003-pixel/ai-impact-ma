# Merger Waves

**The empirically observed phenomenon that M&A activity occurs in distinct cyclical clusters ("waves") rather than being uniformly distributed over time.**

## Summary

Merger waves are a foundational empirical observation in M&A research: deal volumes cluster in boom periods separated by troughs, often correlated with equity market conditions, credit availability, industry shocks, or technological change. Understanding merger waves is Thema 1 of the SS 2026 seminar at Goethe-Universitat Frankfurt. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]

The seminar treats merger waves as the primary empirical phenomenon motivating the theoretical explanations explored in Thema 1 (M&A strategies and theoretical frameworks). Key readings assigned include Bower (2001), Christensen et al. (2011), Trautwein (1990), and Rabier (2017). [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]

Historically, six major merger waves have been identified in the U.S. economy, each associated with distinct industry dynamics: the 1890s horizontal consolidation wave, the 1920s vertical integration wave, the 1960s conglomerate wave, the 1980s hostile takeover wave, the 1990s cross-border strategic wave, and the 2000s private equity wave. A seventh wave driven by digital transformation and AI acquisition activity is debated in contemporary literature.

## Variations / sub-concepts

- Industry-level merger waves (sector-specific clustering)
- Cross-border merger waves
- Technology-driven acquisition waves (e.g., AI acquisition boom)

## Key claims across sources

- Merger waves are the primary empirical phenomenon that M&A theory must explain; they are not random but structured around economic and market cycles. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]
- The Goethe-Universitat seminar places merger waves at the beginning of the M&A framework (Thema 1), establishing them as the empirical foundation before process and integration topics are addressed. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]
- M&A activity exhibits cyclicality in the managerial sentiment dimension: very high positive M&A sentiment (MASS in top quintile) is associated with fewer deals 18 months later, consistent with late-cycle mean-reversion dynamics analogous to equity markets. [[2026-05-03-degen-et-al-2024-llms-ma-forecasting]]
- The OECD Business Confidence Index alone predicts 49.1% of out-of-sample variance in monthly M&A deal counts at an 18-month lag, quantifying how strongly macroeconomic conditions drive merger waves. [[2026-05-03-degen-et-al-2024-llms-ma-forecasting]]
- LLM-derived managerial sentiment (MASS) provides incremental explanatory power for aggregate deal volume above established fundamental drivers including interest rates, VIX, stock market valuation, leverage, and OECD BCI. [[2026-05-03-degen-et-al-2024-llms-ma-forecasting]]
