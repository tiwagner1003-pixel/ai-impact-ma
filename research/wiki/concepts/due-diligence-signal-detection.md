# Due Diligence as Signal Detection

**A decision-theoretic view of DD in which negative findings are noisy signals and acquirers must balance false-alarm risk against the risk of ignoring true warning signs.**

## Summary

Puranam, Powell & Singh (2006) model DD as decision-making under uncertainty. DD does not merely produce facts; it produces noisy signals about whether a target is overvalued or value-destroying. Acquirers must choose whether to continue, renegotiate, or withdraw while balancing Type I errors (abandoning a good deal) and Type II errors (continuing a bad deal). [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]

This lens is useful for AI-assisted DD because it separates two quality questions: (1) does AI detect more relevant signals, and (2) do human decision makers interpret and act on those signals appropriately? AI may improve the first without automatically solving the second. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]

## Key claims across sources

- DD failure includes both failure to discover negative information and failure to respond appropriately after discovering it. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- Negative DD information is probabilistic and noisy, so persistence with a deal can be rational ex ante even if it looks wrong ex post. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- The costs of Type I and Type II errors shape how decision makers react to negative DD information. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- A DD quality framework should therefore include decision-usefulness, not only search accuracy. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
