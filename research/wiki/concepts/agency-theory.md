# Agency Theory

**A theoretical framework explaining how conflicts of interest and information asymmetry between a principal (who delegates) and an agent (who acts) generate agency costs and distort decision-making.**

## Summary

Agency theory, developed by Jensen and Meckling (1976) and elaborated in Eisenhardt (1989), is the most widely used theoretical perspective on information asymmetry in management research: 36% of the 223 articles reviewed by Bergh et al. (2019) adopt it as their primary theoretical lens. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

The theory posits that principals (e.g., shareholders, acquirers) cannot perfectly observe agent behavior (e.g., managers, target-firm executives), creating information asymmetry that gives rise to two problems. The *ex ante* adverse selection problem arises before contracting: the principal cannot verify agent quality or intentions. The *ex post* moral hazard problem arises after contracting: agents can exploit their information advantage to shirk, withhold negative information, or pursue self-interested strategies. In M&A, both problems are active — the acquirer cannot know the true quality of the target's management or hidden liabilities (adverse selection), and post-acquisition integration may be undermined by target management acting in self-interest (moral hazard). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

The standard agency resolutions — monitoring, outcome-based contracts, information disclosure — map directly onto the Bergh et al. (2019) resolution taxonomy: precontractual safeguards and postcontractual monitoring can be used complementarily to attenuate agency costs. In M&A, contingent earnouts are a canonical precommitment device (Shen & Reuer 2005), and board monitoring of acquisition decisions is a governance-level check. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

## Key claims across sources

- Agency theory is the most widely used theoretical lens for information asymmetry in management research (36% of 223 reviewed articles). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- When the principal recognizes that the agent has hidden information that may allow opportunistic behavior, precontractual safeguards and postcontractual monitoring can be used complementarily to attenuate negative effects (Eisenhardt 1989). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Monitoring effectiveness is limited when agent autonomy and specialized information are high; principals then rely on incentive systems to align agent and principal preferences. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
