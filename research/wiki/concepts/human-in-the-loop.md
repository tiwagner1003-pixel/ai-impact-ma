# Human-in-the-Loop

**Governance design in which a human monitors, adjusts, or overrides automated decision support instead of delegating the decision fully to AI.**

## Summary

Human-in-the-loop is widely proposed as a safeguard for AI governance, but Sele & Chugunova (2024) show that it is not automatically quality-improving. Their experiment finds that allowing humans to adjust algorithmic recommendations increases algorithm uptake but decreases decision accuracy because users follow algorithmic advice closely and fail to correct the least accurate recommendations. [[2026-05-07-sele-chugunova-2024-human-in-loop-adm]]

In M&A DD, this means human review should be framed as a quality condition only if it is substantive: reviewers need training, time, authority, and audit procedures to challenge AI outputs. A nominal sign-off layer may produce false comfort and [[automation-bias]]. [[2026-05-07-sele-chugunova-2024-human-in-loop-adm]]

## Key claims across sources

- Human oversight is a common regulatory and governance response to automated decision-making. [[2026-05-07-sele-chugunova-2024-human-in-loop-adm]]
- Human-in-the-loop can increase willingness to use algorithmic decision support. [[2026-05-07-sele-chugunova-2024-human-in-loop-adm]]
- Human-in-the-loop can reduce final decision accuracy if humans over-rely on algorithmic recommendations and fail to correct large errors. [[2026-05-07-sele-chugunova-2024-human-in-loop-adm]]
- For AI in M&A, human oversight must be combined with governance processes such as documentation, validation, and accountability. [[2026-05-04-herbosch-mertens-2025-risk-allocation-ai-ma]]
