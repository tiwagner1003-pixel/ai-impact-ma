# Digital Transformation

**The process by which a firm integrates digital technologies — including big data analytics, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and digital platforms — into its operations, processes, and strategic decision-making, typically measured by the frequency of digital-related disclosures in annual reports.**

## Summary

Digital transformation (DT) broadly describes the adoption and embedding of digital technologies at the enterprise level. In the empirical finance literature, DT is operationalised primarily through text mining of corporate disclosures: firms that make more frequent reference to digital technologies in their annual reports are treated as more digitally advanced. This proxy, while indirect, has been validated against patent-based, asset-based, personnel-based, and index-based measures in the Chinese listed-company literature. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

The mechanisms through which DT affects corporate behaviour are diverse. At the information level, DT enhances a firm's ability to collect, process, and analyse large datasets — reducing its information disadvantages relative to other market participants. At the process level, DT streamlines internal reporting, improves transparency, and reduces friction in information transmission between management and shareholders. At the strategic level, DT enables firms to identify market opportunities (including acquisition targets) more efficiently and to evaluate them with greater accuracy. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

In the M&A context, Zhang et al. (2025) find that DT exerts a positive net effect on M&A activity, primarily through reducing external information asymmetry between acquirers and targets. The effect is heterogeneous: it is larger for non-high-tech firms (which lack pre-existing information systems) and politically unconnected firms (which lack privileged information access), and smaller in regions with already-dense internet infrastructure. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

## Variations / sub-concepts

- Big data analytics — structured data processing at scale
- Artificial intelligence (AI) — machine learning and pattern recognition enabling predictive analytics
- Cloud computing — on-demand computing infrastructure enabling data sharing and collaboration
- Digital platforms — networked intermediaries facilitating information exchange
- Keyword-based DT measurement — log(1 + annual report keyword frequency) as the primary proxy in Chinese firm studies

## Key claims across sources

- Corporate digital transformation significantly promotes M&A behavior, increasing both the likelihood of engaging in deals and the volume and magnitude of transactions, primarily by reducing external information asymmetry. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The overall level of corporate digital transformation in Chinese listed companies shows an upward trend from 2008 to 2022, indicating firms progressively enhance their use of digital technologies, though the gap in digital capabilities among firms is gradually widening. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Digital transformation is validated through five alternative measures: keyword frequency in MD&A section (DT1), digital economy patents filed (DT2), total intangible assets related to digitalization (DT3), number of executives with digital background (DT4), and the CSMAR digital transformation index (DT5) — all yield consistent results. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The Broadband China policy (2013 government initiative to upgrade internet infrastructure in 39 pilot cities from 2014) is an exogenous policy shock that can serve as an alternative instrument for corporate digital transformation at the regional level. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Historical postal and telecommunications infrastructure data (number of landline telephones per province in 1984) serves as a valid instrument for firm-level DT, satisfying both relevance and exclusion restriction requirements. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

## Open questions

- Is "digital transformation" (broad) a valid proxy for "AI adoption" (specific) for the purposes of the Thema 7 seminar paper? The paper uses DT as an umbrella that includes AI but is not limited to it.
- Does the direction of causality fully run from DT to M&A, or do firms planning acquisitions accelerate their digitalization in preparation?
- Are DT effects on M&A symmetric across deal sizes — or does DT only matter for large, complex transactions where information asymmetry is most severe?
