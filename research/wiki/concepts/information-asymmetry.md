# Information Asymmetry

**A situation in which one party to a transaction has materially more or better information than the other party, creating conditions for adverse selection and moral hazard that distort decision-making and pricing.**

## Summary

Information asymmetry is a foundational concept in economics and finance, originally formalised by Akerlof (1970) in "The Market for Lemons," which demonstrated that asymmetric information between buyers and sellers can cause market breakdown or severe underpricing. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]] Myers and Majluf (1984) extended the framework to corporate finance, showing that managers with private information about firm quality will behave differently from what uninformed shareholders would prefer — especially in investment and financing decisions. [[2026-05-05-myers-majluf-1984-corporate-financing-investment-decisions]]

In the M&A context, information asymmetry operates along two distinct axes. **External information asymmetry** exists between the acquiring firm and the target: the buyer cannot accurately assess the target's true value, leading to miscalculations in valuation, adverse selection (buyers may not know what defects are being hidden), and moral hazard (sellers may overstate strengths). This form of asymmetry is the primary barrier to deal initiation and pricing accuracy, and is the mechanism that digital transformation most directly addresses. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

**Internal information asymmetry** exists between a firm's management and its shareholders. When managers hold private information about the firm's prospects, they may initiate M&A transactions that serve their personal interests — power expansion, compensation growth, or empire-building — rather than shareholder value maximisation (Mueller 1969, principal-agent theory). Digital transformation reduces this form of asymmetry by improving transparency in reporting and information flows, which paradoxically can inhibit M&A driven by managerial self-interest. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

Zhang et al. (2025) provide the most direct empirical treatment of both channels in the M&A context, using Chinese listed-company data (2008–2022) to show that the two mechanisms produce opposing effects on M&A activity — and that the external channel dominates the internal channel, yielding a net positive effect of digital transformation on M&A. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]

## Variations / sub-concepts

- External information asymmetry (acquirer–target asymmetry)
- Internal information asymmetry (principal-agent / management–shareholder asymmetry)
- [[adverse-selection]] — the pre-contractual (ex ante) problem where one side hides unfavourable characteristics
- [[moral-hazard]] — the post-contractual (ex post) problem where one side exploits information advantages at the other's expense
- [[information-impactedness]] — Williamson's TCE formulation: asymmetry raises transaction costs because informational parity is costly to achieve
- Agency costs (AC, AC2, AC3 measures in Zhang et al. 2025) — proxies for degree of internal information asymmetry
- [[market-for-lemons]] — the canonical adverse-selection mechanism under quality uncertainty
- [[pecking-order-theory]] — corporate-finance implication of managerial private information

## Key claims across sources

- Digital transformation significantly promotes corporate M&A primarily by reducing external information asymmetry between acquirers and targets. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Akerlof (1970) shows that quality uncertainty can reduce both average market quality and market size when buyers cannot distinguish high- from low-quality sellers. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]]
- Myers & Majluf (1984) show that managerial private information can distort investment and financing decisions, including cases where firms pass up positive-NPV projects. [[2026-05-05-myers-majluf-1984-corporate-financing-investment-decisions]]
- Digital transformation also reduces internal information asymmetry (management–shareholder), which has the opposite effect of inhibiting self-interested, agency-driven M&A. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- The net effect on M&A is positive — external information asymmetry reduction dominates — indicating that digital tools primarily help acquirers understand targets better rather than primarily constraining managerial discretion. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Cross-regional and cross-industry M&A are significantly promoted by digital transformation while local and same-industry M&A are not — because geographically and industrially distant deals face higher external information asymmetry. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Equity M&A is significantly promoted by digital transformation; asset M&A is not — because equity deals require comprehensive evaluation of a target's full profile (higher external information demands) while asset deals are more narrowly scoped. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Non-high-tech firms and firms lacking political connections benefit most from digital transformation's information asymmetry reduction — both groups that previously had limited information access channels. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Higher regional internet development attenuates the M&A-promoting effect of firm-level digital transformation (negative moderation), suggesting diminishing returns when external information infrastructure is already strong. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- A key limitation in GenAI due diligence is that targets are unlikely to grant acquirers access to their internal data for input through GenAI tools — an information asymmetry constraint that persists even with advanced AI tools. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- Bergh et al. (2019) identify five distinct conceptualizations of information asymmetry in management research: private information, different information, hidden information (adverse selection/moral hazard), lack of perfect information, and information impactedness — each carrying different theoretical and resolution implications. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Information asymmetry plays four roles in management theory: as an assumption (most common), mechanism, construct, and boundary condition; the last three roles are underexplored and represent the primary gap in the literature. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Three antecedent conditions generate information asymmetry: (a) unobservable or uncertain qualities of actors/products/services, (b) structural barriers to information propagation, and (c) strategic and behavioral barriers to information sharing. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Five primary resolution mechanisms reduce information asymmetry: incentives to gather/disclose, precommitment, monitoring and rewards, conveying information via actions (signaling), and information intermediaries; three strategies exploit it: concealment, impression management, and decoupling. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Focal actors' intent moderates resolution selection: reduce-reduce settings (both parties want less asymmetry) favor joint value creation; reduce-increase settings (one exploits) create contested outcomes; increase-increase settings (both seek causal ambiguity) produce individual value protection. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

## Open questions

- Does the dual-mechanism framework (external promotes, internal inhibits) generalise beyond Chinese listed companies to Western M&A contexts with different governance and disclosure regimes?
- At what level of digital maturity does the external-channel benefit saturate — i.e., is there a ceiling effect?
- How does LLM-based information extraction (e.g., MASS index from earnings calls) interact with firm-level digital transformation as complementary or substitutable mechanisms for reducing information asymmetry?
- What is the relationship between information asymmetry reduction and M&A deal quality (post-deal returns) — does less asymmetry always produce better outcomes?
- Can AI tools reduce all five conceptualizations (private information, different information, hidden information, lack of perfect information, impactedness) — or do they address only some, leaving others intact or even worsened?
- Does the opacity of AI outputs (the black-box problem) create a new form of information asymmetry between the AI system and its users, paradoxically re-introducing asymmetry while reducing another?
