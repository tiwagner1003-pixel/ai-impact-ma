# Due Diligence

**The pre-acquisition investigation and verification process in which a buyer systematically examines a target company's financials, legal position, operations, and risks before closing a deal.**

## Summary

In the M&A seminar framework, due diligence falls within the "Wertberechnung" (value calculation) half of the deal process, together with takeover tactics and synergy calculation. It precedes post-merger integration in the sequential process. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]

Due diligence is covered in Thema 3 of the SS 2026 seminar ("Due Diligence und Takeover-Taktiken"), and is a key phase where AI tools (natural language processing, anomaly detection, document automation) are being deployed — making it directly relevant to Thema 7 as well. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]

## Key claims across sources

- Due diligence sits in the "Wertberechnung" phase alongside synergy calculation, distinguished from "Wertschaffung" (post-merger integration). [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]
- AI-powered software can rapidly analyse large volumes of contracts and legal papers, spotting important clauses, pitfalls, and business prospects, thereby lowering the possibility of human error in due diligence. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- During the merger phase, AI combines and analyses data from financial, legal, tax, HR, IT, and environmental domains simultaneously, enabling more precise risk assessments and easier integration planning. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- Due diligence and negotiation are frequently conducted concurrently, allowing investigational data to feed directly into the development of pertinent contractual clauses. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- The broad due diligence scope in M&A ranges from financial and legal to commercial; each focus area has distinct end goals — financial DD aims to confirm profitability and provide a valuation basis; legal DD validates contractual agreements and determines warranties and indemnities. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- AI automates document review, data analysis, and risk assessment in due diligence, allowing practitioners to concentrate on critical strategic decisions and creative problem solving rather than administrative document handling. [[2026-05-04-bremen-2024-ai-accelerates-ma]]
- Many high-profile deal failures can be traced back to poor due diligence, hasty decision making, and critical financial mistakes — a key motivation for deploying AI to ensure appropriate diligence coverage before sign-off. [[2026-05-04-bremen-2024-ai-accelerates-ma]]
- AI prompt engineering is used in diligence to focus review on key risks, detect unique patterns or discrepancies, and identify risk-reduction opportunities. [[2026-05-04-bremen-2024-ai-accelerates-ma]]
- Among M&A practitioners currently using generative AI, 58% apply it specifically to the due diligence phase — the highest adoption rate of any single deal phase. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- GenAI can be trained to parse material contracts and identify deviations from a model contract, saving time and directing reviewers toward problematic areas; missing a critical fact in due diligence can be costly. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- A key limitation: targets are unlikely to grant potential acquirers access to their internal data for input through GenAI tools, constraining GenAI's utility in the later valuation and due diligence stages where proprietary target data is needed. [[2026-05-04-bain-2024-genai-ma-hope-meets-hype]]
- External information asymmetry — the principal challenge that due diligence addresses — is significantly reduced by firm-level digital transformation, which enhances acquirers' ability to collect and analyse information about targets via digital platforms, industry reports, and online databases. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- In the information asymmetry framework (Bergh et al. 2019), due diligence functions simultaneously as a "gathering and disclosure" resolution (incentivising information revelation by the target), a "monitoring" resolution (verifying claims through third-party review), and a vehicle for deploying "information intermediaries" (advisors, auditors) — making it the most theoretically composite resolution mechanism in M&A. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Contingent earnouts — a common contractual element in M&A — are classified by Bergh et al. (2019) as a "precommitment" resolution that transfers ownership from seller to buyer in gradients over time, reducing acquirer overpayment risk under conditions of external information asymmetry. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Systematic-review evidence confirms that DD is broader than legal/financial review: relevant DD areas include financial, legal, tax, environmental, operational, market, HR, cultural, strategic, marketing, IP, technology, and R&D. [[2026-05-07-bhagwan-et-al-2018-systematic-review-dd-ma]]
- DD quality depends not only on finding information but on interpreting and acting on it; acquirers can fail DD by ignoring or underweighting negative but noisy findings. [[2026-05-07-puranam-et-al-2006-dd-signal-detection]]
- Legal DD is a high-recall information retrieval problem: missing a key clause or risk can be more damaging than reviewing additional false positives. [[2026-05-07-dwivedi-kamps-2025-icl-due-diligence]]
- Across all 50 KIRA DD topics, few-shot LLMs achieve acceptable recall without labeled training data; Dolphin-Llama3 reaches 0.926 recall, Gemma2 0.873, Llama3.1 0.818 — compared to CRF-PA's 0.847–0.851. [[2026-05-07-dwivedi-kamps-2025-icl-due-diligence]]
- Detailed DD topic descriptions written for human legal annotators transfer directly to LLM prompts and are the single most important prompt component for legal DD classification. [[2026-05-07-dwivedi-kamps-2025-icl-due-diligence]]
- Open-source LLMs run locally via Ollama offer a data-privacy-preserving path to confidential legal DD automation without sending documents to cloud APIs. [[2026-05-07-dwivedi-kamps-2025-icl-due-diligence]]

## Classical DD process baseline

Howson (2003) provides the canonical codification of the classical (pre-AI) DD process. His five-strand programme model — (1) asset/liability verification, (2) risk identification, (3) negotiation protection, (4) synergy identification, (5) post-acquisition planning — remains the standard against which AI-assisted DD is benchmarked. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]

- DD is "one of the most important but least well understood aspects of the acquisition process" — it is not a chore for accountants and lawyers but must be planned and professionally managed. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- The true acquisition failure rate is approximately 75%; the primary cause is over-focus on financials at the expense of other DD disciplines. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- Pre-acquisition DD "should be constructed to give as good an insight as possible into post-deal implementation as to isolating pre-deal risks" — the fifth strand (post-acquisition planning) is the most neglected in practice. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- For the DD-focused research question, Howson is the strongest baseline for the "classical DD" side of the process comparison: his weaknesses are not just speed and manual workload, but also process-governance risks such as adviser silos, unclear mandates, and weak reporting lines. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- A 1999 KPMG study cited by Howson found an 83% correlation between integration implementation success and overall acquisition success, with pre-deal synergy evaluation (+28% vs. average) as the top hard key to success. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
- AI tools currently automate only the legal and financial document-review strands of classical DD (strands 1–2 for financial/legal, partially strand 3); management, HR, commercial, environmental, and IP disciplines remain human-led. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]

## NLP and LLM approaches to due diligence automation

Jang & Stikkel (2024) provide the first empirical study applying PLMs and LLMs directly to the due diligence problem, using the [[kira-dataset]]. They formalise due diligence as a binary sequential classification task: identifying which sentences in a legal document are relevant for a given topic. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]

- Due diligence documents are exceptionally long (avg. 3,308 sentences) with very sparse relevant labels (avg. 4.8 relevant sentences per document, >99.8% non-relevant), creating a class imbalance challenge that defeats standard NLP approaches. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]
- A [[hierarchical-sentence-extraction]] architecture (Sentence-BERT sentence encoder + Bi-LSTM document encoder) is the most suitable and practically efficient model for DD, achieving higher recall than the CRF baseline on four of five topics. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]
- Legal-domain PLMs ([[legalbert]]) do not reliably outperform general-domain BERT on due diligence classification; the task structure (sequential sentence context) matters more than encoder domain-specificity. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]
- GPT-4 (few-shot, 2–8 examples) achieves F1 of 0.81–0.82 on one KIRA topic, with recall of 0.93–0.96, demonstrating practical utility as a high-recall first-pass screening tool for lawyers. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]
- A two-stage pipeline — LLM for high-recall paragraph-level filtering, followed by a precision-oriented discriminative model for exact sentence selection — is proposed as the optimal practical architecture. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]
- The [[kira-dataset]] is the only publicly available DD dataset; its restricted access is identified as the primary bottleneck for research progress in NLP for M&A due diligence. [[2026-05-04-jang-stikkel-2024-nlp-llms-due-diligence]]
- [[maud-dataset]] complements KIRA by benchmarking reading comprehension over public merger agreements rather than sentence-level relevance detection in due diligence documents. [[2026-05-06-wang-et-al-2023-maud-merger-agreement-understanding]]
- MAUD provides a more direct error-rate style benchmark for merger-agreement review: the best multi-task LegalBERT baseline reaches 76.1% micro-F1 and 59.7% macro-F1, useful for assistance but not sufficient for autonomous legal review. [[2026-05-06-wang-et-al-2023-maud-merger-agreement-understanding]]
- The most difficult MAUD categories include Conditions to Closing, Deal Protection and Related Provisions, and Material Adverse Effect clauses, showing that legally important, context-heavy provisions remain hard for current models. [[2026-05-06-wang-et-al-2023-maud-merger-agreement-understanding]]
