# Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A)

**The field covering corporate combination transactions (mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures) including strategy, deal process, valuation, and post-deal integration.**

## Summary

M&A is a core discipline in corporate strategy and finance, encompassing why firms combine (strategy and theory), how deals are executed (process, due diligence, takeover tactics), how value is estimated and distributed (valuation, synergy calculation), and how organizational integration is managed after closing (post-merger integration). [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]

The Goethe-Universitat Frankfurt seminar framework for SS 2026 organizes M&A knowledge into three layers: (I) deal types and strategic rationale, (II) the sequential transaction process divided into value calculation (Wertberechnung: due diligence + synergies) and value creation (Wertschaffung: PMI), and (III) success measurement. Cross-cutting influences — currently AI and ESG — are studied as horizontal themes applied across this entire framework. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]

Haleblian et al. (2009) provide the strongest general M&A review currently in the wiki, showing that acquisition research is spread across management, finance, economics, accounting, and sociology and that theoretical integration remains incomplete. [[2026-05-05-haleblian-et-al-2009-ma-review]]

## Key claims across sources

- M&A success measurement is a distinct topic requiring its own methods, separate from the deal execution phase. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]
- The seminar framework separates "Wertberechnung" (value calculation: due diligence and synergy estimation) from "Wertschaffung" (value creation: post-merger integration) as conceptually distinct phases. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]
- AI and ESG are positioned as cross-cutting topics that span the entire M&A process, not siloed to a single phase. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-intro]]
- A merger requires two or more businesses to combine to form a new entity or holding company; an acquisition involves buying stocks or other assets from another business to gain managerial control, which may not always be amicably carried out. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- The M&A process in India is formulated in five stages: (1) M&A strategy formulation, (2) target identification and initial assessment, (3) target valuation and due diligence, (4) deal structure negotiation and closing, (5) post-merger integration — with integration being the determining factor in M&A success. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- Over 50% of M&A transactions fail to produce expected results; failures arise from both hard factors (transaction structuring, poor financing) and soft "human factors" (communication, cultural compatibility, key employee retention). [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- Traditional M&A methodology has not evolved for decades and is frequently criticised for being demanding, time-consuming, and expensive — motivating the adoption of AI and lean process improvements. [[2026-05-04-singh-2023-ai-transformative-potential-ma]]
- Corporate digital transformation significantly promotes M&A behavior (deal likelihood, volume, and magnitude) among Chinese A-share listed companies (2008–2022, N=22,274), primarily by reducing external information asymmetry between acquirers and target firms. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Information asymmetry in M&A operates along two axes: external (acquirer–target, promoting adverse selection and miscalculation) and internal (management–shareholder, enabling self-interested agency M&A); digital transformation attenuates both, with the external channel dominating. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- Cross-regional and cross-industry M&A are more strongly promoted by digital transformation than local or same-industry M&A, confirming that information disadvantage (not transaction costs alone) is the primary barrier to geographically and industrially distant deals. [[2026-05-04-zhang-et-al-2025-digital-transformation-ma-information-asymmetry]]
- M&A research has repeatedly found weak or negative average acquirer performance, while target shareholders often gain through acquisition premiums. [[2026-05-05-haleblian-et-al-2009-ma-review]]
- King et al. (2004) meta-analyze 93 studies and find that acquisitions do not, on average, positively change acquiring-firm performance. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
- The standard practitioner model of the M&A acquisition process comprises eight phases in circular order: Strategy (deciding whether and how to buy or sell), Valuation (determining the company's value), Financing (obtaining funds), Structuring (making accounting, legal, and tax designations), Due Diligence (verifying claims and discovering risk exposures), Negotiation (convincing the other party to agree to terms), Closing (consummating the transaction), and Integration (managing postmerger operations). The circular diagram signals that integration feeds back into future strategy. [[2026-05-07-lajoux-2024-art-of-ma-sixth-edition-ch1-ch6]]
- "Merger" and "acquisition" are legally distinct: "acquisition" is the generic term for ownership transfer (stock or asset purchase); "merger" is the narrow statutory procedure in which one corporation is absorbed into and legally disappears into another. The colloquial equation of the two terms is a misnomer driven by boardroom politics and public messaging, not legal precision. [[2026-05-07-lajoux-2024-art-of-ma-sixth-edition-ch1-ch6]]

## Canonical practitioner references

Two standard practitioner references anchor the M&A process literature used in the Thema 7 seminar paper:

- Lajoux (2024) *The Art of M&A* (6th edn., McGraw-Hill Education) — the broadest single reference covering the full lifecycle in Q&A format; the sixth and final edition in the series. Chapter 1 provides the canonical eight-phase circular M&A lifecycle diagram: Strategy → Valuation → Financing → Structuring → Due Diligence → Negotiation → Closing → Integration. Serves as the primary Kap. 2.1 lifecycle reference, superseding the 2019 preview. [[2026-05-07-lajoux-2024-art-of-ma-sixth-edition-ch1-ch6]]
- Howson (2003) *Due Diligence: The Critical Stage in M&A* (Gower) — the specialist reference for the DD phase; provides the five-strand model and 16-discipline taxonomy that serves as the pre-AI baseline in Abschnitt 3.2. [[2026-05-07-howson-2003-due-diligence-critical-stage]]
