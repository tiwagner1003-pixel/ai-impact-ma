# Adverse Selection

**The ex ante problem arising when one party to a transaction has hidden information about its own quality or intentions before contracting, causing the uninformed party to make suboptimal partner or asset selections.**

## Summary

Adverse selection was formalized by Akerlof (1970) in "The Market for Lemons": because buyers cannot distinguish high- from low-quality used cars, they offer only average-quality prices, which drives high-quality sellers out of the market and degrades average market quality until the market potentially collapses. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]]

In management research, adverse selection is the primary ex ante framing of "hidden information" — the conceptualization of information asymmetry associated with agency theory. It arises whenever qualities of actors, products, or services cannot be directly observed before commitment. The information about quality and performance characteristics of applicable assets "is not common knowledge, and any information provided by a present owner may be opportunistically biased, causing adverse selection problems" (Vanhaverbeke et al. 2002, quoted in Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

In M&A, adverse selection is the defining pre-deal risk: the acquirer cannot know whether it is buying a "lemon" (a target that conceals defects) or a genuinely high-quality firm. Due diligence, third-party verification (advisors, auditors), and contingent earnouts are all institutional responses to the adverse selection problem. AI-assisted due diligence tools (Jang & Stikkel 2024, Bozman et al. 2026) directly target this problem by automating the extraction and classification of target quality signals from large document sets. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]

## Key claims across sources

- Adverse selection arises from the ex ante problem of "hidden information": information about quality and performance characteristics is not common knowledge and may be opportunistically biased by the informed party (Vanhaverbeke et al. 2002, per Bergh et al. 2019). [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- Akerlof (1970) showed that quality uncertainty can reduce both average market quality and market size when buyers cannot distinguish high- from low-quality sellers. [[2026-05-05-akerlof-1970-market-for-lemons]]
- Unobservable or uncertain qualities — how exchange participants navigate each other's unobservable characteristics — is the most pervasive antecedent condition generating information asymmetry in management research. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
- During hostile takeovers or when firms have no prior experience with a potential partner, potential acquirers cannot conduct due diligence on target assets — the canonical M&A adverse selection context. [[2026-05-07-bergh-et-al-2019-information-asymmetry-management-research]]
