# Fintech Regulation

**The evolving body of federal and state rules governing nondepository financial technology firms that use algorithmic and AI-based platforms to provide financial services including credit, payments, and investment — with particular controversy over the allocation of regulatory authority between the OCC (federal) and state banking supervisors.**

## Summary

Fintech regulation sits at the intersection of banking law, consumer protection, and AI governance. Johnson et al. (2019) provide a detailed analysis of the regulatory landscape as it stood in 2019, focusing on the central tension between federal and state oversight of fintech firms deploying ADM platforms. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]

The U.S. has a "dual banking system" in which both federal and state regulators issue charters and supervise banks. Historically, state regulators have been the primary line of defense for consumers against predatory financial practices — particularly in areas like interest-rate caps, usury laws, and payday lending regulation. The OCC's Fintech Charter Decision (July 2018) threatened to preempt this state-level protection by granting federal SPNB charters to nondepository fintech firms, enabling them to escape the consumer-protection and antidiscrimination laws that states have carefully built over decades. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]

Johnson et al. argue that effective fintech regulation requires three elements: (1) preserving state regulatory authority over fintech firms through coordinated state-federal oversight rather than OCC preemption; (2) requiring algorithmic transparency and explainability as a legal condition of operating ADM platforms in consumer credit markets; and (3) empowering the CFPB and state regulators to establish and enforce a uniform floor of consumer-protection standards. In some instances, they argue, regulators should formally evaluate whether to ban the use of algorithms altogether in certain consumer credit markets where bias risks cannot be sufficiently mitigated. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]

## Variations / sub-concepts

- Federal preemption in fintech — OCC SPNB charters preempting state consumer-protection laws
- SPNB charters (Special Purpose National Bank charters) — the OCC's vehicle for bringing nondepository fintech firms under federal charter
- State-level fintech oversight — state banking supervisors, attorneys general, and financial services regulators (e.g., NYDFS)
- Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) obligations — whether fintech firms should be subject to community investment requirements
- [[automated-decision-making]] — the technology around which fintech regulation debates primarily revolve

## Key claims across sources

- The OCC's Fintech Charter Decision is the agency's first attempt in its 140-year history to charter nondepository institutions as banks, enabling federal preemption of state consumer-protection and antidiscrimination laws. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
- Federal preemption by OCC removes state interest-rate caps, usury laws, and antidiscrimination statutes that are the primary consumer defense in payday lending and consumer credit markets. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
- Coordinated state-federal regulation — not OCC preemption — is the appropriate governance structure for fintech, with states acting as "laboratories of democracy" to quickly respond to emerging algorithmic threats. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
- The CFPB could serve an important role in establishing minimum standards for ADM transparency and explainability but its institutional capacity is vulnerable to political reorganisation. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
- An industrial policy steering underwriting technologies toward forms capable of being regulated and reformed is needed — without such incentives, cherished values of equal opportunity will be further marginalized. [[2026-05-07-johnson-pasquale-chapman-2019-ai-ml-bias-finance]]
