# M&A Success Measurement

**The methods and metrics used to evaluate whether a merger or acquisition created or destroyed value, spanning short-term event studies, long-term accounting measures, and qualitative assessments.**

## Summary

M&A success measurement is Thema 8 of the SS 2026 Goethe-Universitat Frankfurt seminar — the largest single topic by assigned reading count (13 readings). The topic is positioned at the end of the seminar framework as an overarching lens that applies backward across all deal phases. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]

The assigned readings cover the two dominant methodological traditions: event study methodology (measuring short-window abnormal stock returns around deal announcement) and long-run accounting performance analysis. Meta-analyses (Datta et al. 1992; King et al. 2004; King et al. 2021) synthesize large samples of prior studies and consistently find that acquiring firms, on average, earn zero or negative excess returns — a persistent and important empirical regularity. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]

A key methodological debate concerns the appropriate measurement window: Oler et al. (2008) warn that short-window event studies can be misleading for strategic management research, while Zollo & Meier (2008) argue that the very definition of "M&A performance" is contested and multi-dimensional. Papadakis & Thanos (2010) propose using multiple criteria simultaneously. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]

King et al. (2004), now ingested as a primary source, is the strongest direct support in the wiki for the claim that acquisition performance is weak on average and that common antecedents do not fully explain performance variation. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]

## Variations / sub-concepts

- Event study methodology (short-window abnormal returns)
- Long-run stock return studies
- Accounting-based performance measures (ROA, ROE, EBITDA)
- Managerial (subjective) performance assessments
- Innovation and capability-based performance metrics

## Key claims across sources

- Meta-analyses consistently find that acquirer firms earn on average zero or negative excess returns from M&A; target firm shareholders typically gain. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]
- King et al. (2004) specifically find that acquiring firms' performance does not positively change on average as a function of acquisition activity and is modestly negatively affected. [[2026-05-05-king-et-al-2004-post-acquisition-performance-meta-analysis]]
- Haleblian et al. (2009) summarize earlier evidence that targets typically gain while acquirers often experience neutral or negative returns. [[2026-05-05-haleblian-et-al-2009-ma-review]]
- Short-window event studies are the most common method but may not capture the full long-run strategic value or destruction from acquisitions. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]
- M&A performance is a multi-dimensional construct that cannot be captured by a single metric. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]
- M&A success measurement is treated as a structurally distinct topic requiring its own methods, separate from deal execution measurement. [[2026-05-03-bachelorseminar-ma-ss2026-syllabus]]
