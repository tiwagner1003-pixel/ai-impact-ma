# Snowball Method (Schneeballsystem)

**A literature search technique in which one relevant source is used as a seed, its reference list is mined for further sources, and those sources are in turn mined for further references, iterating outward.**

## Summary

The snowball method (Schneeballsystem) starts from a single book or article that is clearly relevant to the research topic. The references in that seed source are examined for further relevant works; those works are obtained and their references examined in turn, and so on. This produces a growing corpus that tends to converge on the core literature of a field. [[2026-05-03-einfuehrung-wiss-arbeiten]]

The method has a clear advantage: it offers a relatively fast path to the canonical literature of a topic. It is particularly well suited for deepening understanding once a topic is established. However, it has structural blind spots: works that are not heavily cited will not be discovered, it carries a risk of citation bias (following the same intellectual lineage), and recent publications may not be captured because they have had less time to accumulate citations. [[2026-05-03-einfuehrung-wiss-arbeiten]]

For these reasons, the snowball method must always be supplemented by systematic database searches. Neither method alone is sufficient. [[2026-05-03-einfuehrung-wiss-arbeiten]]

## Key claims across sources

- The snowball method is best suited for deepening a topic, not for initial systematic coverage. [[2026-05-03-einfuehrung-wiss-arbeiten]]
- Disadvantages: uncited works are missed; risk of citation bias; recent research may not be captured. [[2026-05-03-einfuehrung-wiss-arbeiten]]
- Must be supplemented by systematic database searches (JSTOR, WISO, EconLit, Web of Science, Scopus). [[2026-05-03-einfuehrung-wiss-arbeiten]]
